# 21 — Name and Brand Options

**Version:** 2.2 · **Date:** 21 October 2026 · **Status:** Recommendation. The final choice remains **D-019 (Decision needed)**, after trademark and domain checks.

## The brief (owner)
"This is like a Google… We had built a tool in my office and called it Woogle (Wells Google)… Look at a creative name for this project in that light."

What made "Woogle" work:
- It's a **portmanteau**: the audience (wells) welded onto a verb everyone knows (Google).
- It's **playful and easy to say**.
- **It tells you who it's for.**

This product's "who" is everyone, and its first question is literally *"Who are you?"*. So the creative seed is **who + toolkit / search**.

## Avoid "-oogle" names
Google has a long record of challenging "-oogle" names, from domain disputes to lawsuits (e.g. the Froogles.com case; eWeek's "Google's 'oogle' hunters" coverage). There are also existing products called **Whoogle** (an open-source Google search proxy) and **Hoogle** (a Haskell search engine). An internal nickname is fine, as Woogle was. A public brand should keep the spirit and drop the "-oogle".

## Shortlist
| Name | Built from | Say it | Why it works | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Whoolkit** ⭐ | *Who* + *toolkit* | "WHOOL-kit" | Woogle's DNA: one playful portmanteau that names the audience (**who**ever you are) and the promise (your **toolkit**). Matches the landing question and the main screen ("Your digital toolkit"). A quick web search found no product using the name. | Spelling needs one exposure (say it, then show it). Check the .com, .app and app-store names, and trademark classes 9/41/42. |
| **WhoKit** | *Who* + *kit* | "WHO-kit" | Shorter and instantly readable. | Similar names exist ("WhatKit" toolbox app); weaker distinctiveness. |
| **Kitwho** | Reversed | "KIT-who" | Fun, chant-like. | Reads less naturally. |
| **Toolprint** | *Tool* + *blueprint* | "TOOL-print" | Roadmap and blueprint feel; serious tone for professionals. | Loses the "who"; generic search competition. |
| **Upgrade Me** | The owner's own words: "everyone can get a digital upgrade" | — | Warm, benefit-led. | Two words; hard to trademark; many apps use "Upgrade". |

## Recommendation
**Whoolkit — the digital toolkit for whoever you are.** Keep **Digital Advantage** as the descriptive line or as the organisation or project name, and keep the approved H1 ("Make technology your advantage", D-002), so nothing in the spec has to change:

> **Whoolkit** · *Make technology your advantage.*
> Who are you? → your tools, skills and roadmaps.

**Taglines to test:**
- "Tell us who you are. Get your toolkit."
- "Every job has a digital advantage. Find yours."
- "Become the tech person in your family."

**Internal nickname:** the team can still call the search box "Whoogle" internally, the same way Woogle lived in your office. Just don't ship that name publicly.

## Before adopting (checklist for D-019)
1. Domain availability: whoolkit.com / .app / .org, plus country domains for launch markets (e.g. .ng, .co.uk). This couldn't be checked from here.
2. Trademark search in launch markets: USPTO, UKIPO, EUIPO, WIPO Global Brand Database, and the Nigerian Trademarks Registry. Use classes 9, 41 and 42.
3. App-store and social handle availability (@whoolkit).
4. A five-person say-and-spell test in each launch market. If more than one person misspells it after hearing it, use **WhoKit** instead.
5. Then update `SITE_URL`, the wordmark placeholder (06) and the D-019 row.

Sources consulted: eWeek, "Google's 'Oogle' Hunters Bag Another One"; Out-Law (Pinsent Masons), "Google sues Froogles.com"; web searches for each shortlisted name on 21 Oct 2026 (Whoolkit: no product found; Tooggle and WhatKit exist as apps).
