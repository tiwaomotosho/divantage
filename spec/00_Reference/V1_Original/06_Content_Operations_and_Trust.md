# 06 — Content Operations and Trust

## 1. Editorial standard
Digital Advantage should be useful and honest. Do not publish a tool record merely because a product exists. Every entry should help a person make a decision or complete a task.

## 2. Content sourcing
Preferred sources, depending on the claim:
1. Official product website and documentation.
2. Official pricing page and terms.
3. Official support or availability documentation.
4. Maintained official repositories for open-source products.
5. Credible independent reviews or comparisons for context.
6. Carefully selected tutorials and demonstrations.

Third-party reviews may inform editorial context but must not replace official evidence for pricing or stated product availability when official sources exist.

## 3. Pricing vocabulary
Use explicit controlled values:
- `free`: core described use is available without payment, subject to stated conditions.
- `freemium`: a usable free tier exists alongside paid plans.
- `free_trial`: access is time-limited or trial-based.
- `paid_subscription`: ongoing payment is required for the described use.
- `paid_once`: one-time purchase for the described product/edition.
- `open_source`: source code is available under a stated licence; this does not automatically mean hosting or use has zero cost.
- `usage_based`: cost depends on usage.
- `enterprise_quote`: pricing requires a sales quote.
- `mixed`: different editions or features use materially different models.
- `unknown`: not verified.

Do not use “free” as a substitute for freemium or a free trial. Explain important limits and distinguish product price from hardware, data, hosting, transaction, or API costs.

## 4. Verification workflow
1. Discover candidate.
2. Confirm product identity and official URL.
3. Check product is currently accessible and relevant.
4. Review capability claims against official sources.
5. Verify pricing/access model.
6. Verify platforms and region availability when material.
7. Curate relevant tutorials.
8. Add categories, tasks, alternatives, and limitations.
9. Run duplicate and completeness checks.
10. Reviewer approves publication.
11. Set a review interval based on volatility and impact.
12. Recheck or archive as appropriate.

## 5. Suggested review intervals
These are operational defaults to validate, not guarantees:
- Pricing and plan limits: every 30–90 days or when flagged.
- Product availability and major capabilities: every 90–180 days.
- Tutorials and external links: automated link checks regularly; human relevance review based on importance.
- Stable conceptual guides: every 6–12 months or when flagged.
- High-impact privacy/security claims: more frequent review and specialist review where appropriate.

High-volatility products should be reviewed sooner. Broken links should enter a queue rather than be silently ignored.

## 6. Editorial status and confidence
- Draft: incomplete, not public.
- In review: awaiting review.
- Published: meets publication requirements.
- Needs review: potentially stale or flagged.
- Archived: no longer recommended or available.

Confidence should refer to evidence quality, not a vague claim that a product is “good.” Keep source notes visible to editors. On public pages, show the last review date and appropriate caveats.

## 7. Tutorial curation
For every external tutorial:
- Confirm the link works.
- Identify publisher and format.
- Summarise what the learner will gain.
- Record language and cost status when known.
- Check whether it teaches a relevant, current workflow.
- Avoid implying endorsement by the tutorial publisher.
- Provide an alternative when a critical resource becomes unavailable.

Do not copy or republish third-party videos or course material without permission. Link to the source and respect copyright and platform terms.

## 8. Corrections and user feedback
Provide a “Report an issue” link on relevant pages. The form should capture:
- page/resource URL;
- issue type;
- optional explanation;
- optional contact details;
- privacy notice.

Do not require an account to report an error. Rate-limit and moderate submissions. A correction is not automatically accepted as fact; editors verify it before changing the public record.

## 9. Sponsorship and affiliate policy
If sponsorships or affiliate links are ever introduced:
- disclose them clearly near the relevant content;
- do not let payment silently determine rankings;
- separate commercial placement from editorial recommendations;
- document the policy publicly;
- preserve non-commercial ways to discover alternatives.

## 10. AI-assisted content operations
AI may assist with candidate discovery, summarisation, duplicate detection, classification suggestions, and change detection. It must not independently certify accuracy or publish sensitive claims without review.

For AI-generated summaries:
- preserve source links;
- compare claims with source material;
- avoid inventing pricing or features;
- record editor approval;
- provide a correction path.

## 11. Quality checklist before publication
- Does this help solve a defined task?
- Is the title understandable to a beginner?
- Is the official URL confirmed?
- Are pricing labels accurate and sourced?
- Are platform/region claims verified or clearly unknown?
- Are limitations explained?
- Are tutorials relevant and working?
- Are related links useful?
- Is the record distinct from existing entries?
- Is the review date present?
- Is the language clear, inclusive, and non-promotional?
